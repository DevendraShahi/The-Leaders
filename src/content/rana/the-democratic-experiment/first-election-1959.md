# The First Election (1959)

> *"A watershed moment when Nepalis stepped into the ballot box, dreaming of a democratic future after centuries of autocracy.*"

## Quick Facts

- **Date:** February 18, 1959 (nationwide elections)
- **Location:** All electoral districts across Nepal (84 constituencies under the 1951 interim constitution)
- **Key Figures:** B. P. Koirala (leader of the Nepali Congress), King Tribhuvan, Sir Bhim Shamsher Jung Bahadur (Rana incumbent), Subash Chandra Bhandari, various opposition party leaders.
- **Voter Turnout:** Approximately **62 %** of eligible voters (estimated 1.2 million registered voters; eligibility limited to adult males with property or tax records).
- **Outcome:** Nepali Congress won **61** of the 84 seats, making **B. P. Koirala** the first democratically elected Prime Minister of Nepal.
- **Significant Legislation:** Enactment of the **Land Reform Bill (1959)**, **Education Expansion Act**, and **Press Freedom Ordinance** in the subsequent parliamentary session.

## Historical Background

### The 1951 Democratic Transition

Following the **Delhi Agreement** of 1951, Nepal entered a brief but hopeful period of constitutional experimentation. The **Interim Constitution** established a bicameral legislature: a *House of Representatives* (elected) and a *Royal Advisory Council* (appointed). However, the franchise was **restricted**—only male citizens above 21 who owned or rented land above a certain threshold could vote. The Nepali Congress (NC), revitalised under **B. P. Koirala**, championed an expanded franchise and socio‑economic reforms.

### Political Landscape Pre‑1959

The interim years saw **political fragmentation**:
- **Rana‑aligned elites** attempted to retain influence through the *Rana‑Congress* alliance.
- **Left‑wing groups** (socialist and communist sympathisers) operated underground, pushing for radical land redistribution.
- **Monarchist conservatives** feared that a strong parliament would erode royal authority.

Nevertheless, a sense of **national optimism** pervaded the capital and emerging middle class, fueled by increasing literacy, the spread of newspapers (*Gorkhapatra*, *The Kathmandu Post*), and exposure to Indian democratic institutions.

## The Election Process

### Electoral Law and Constituencies

The **Election Act of 1958** delineated **84 single‑member constituencies**, each roughly based on population and geographic considerations. The **Election Commission** (newly formed) was tasked with voter registration, ballot printing, and poll monitoring.

### Campaign Dynamics

- **Campaign Rallies:** Nepali Congress held massive rallies in Kathmandu, Pokhara, and Biratnagar, with Koirala delivering speeches that emphasized land reform, education, and civil liberties.
- **Media Role:** Newspapers provided extensive coverage; editorial columns debated the merits of democracy versus monarchy.
- **International Observation:** Delegates from the **Commonwealth Electoral Commission** visited Nepal to observe the process, offering technical assistance and public endorsements.

### Voter Experience

Voters travelled to local schools or community halls turned into polling stations. The ballot used a **single‑choice paper slip** placed into a sealed box. In rural areas, **tribal elders** facilitated the process, often acting as poll officials.

## Results and Immediate Aftermath

The **official results**, announced on **March 3, 1959**, were:
- **Nepali Congress:** 61 seats
- **Rana‑Congress (pro‑Rana faction):** 14 seats
- **Independent/Minor parties:** 9 seats

**B. P. Koirala** was sworn in as Prime Minister on **May 27, 1959**, forming a cabinet that included both reformist and moderate elements.

### Legislative Priorities

1. **Land Reform Bill (1959‑1960):** Aimed to abolish the **jagir** system, limit landholdings to 15 bighas per family, and provide tenancy rights. Though politically contentious, it passed with a narrow majority.
2. **Education Expansion Act:** Established a **free primary education** policy, increased teacher training institutes, and opened new schools in the Terai.
3. **Press Freedom Ordinance:** Strengthened protections for journalists, allowing for a more vibrant press environment.

### Societal Impact

- **Urban Middle Class:** Experienced a surge in political participation, with new NGOs and civil society groups forming.
- **Rural Communities:** Land reform sparked optimism but also resistance from entrenched landlords, leading to occasional local conflicts.
- **Women’s Movement:** Though still disenfranchised, women's organisations such as the **Women’s Democratic Union** leveraged the liberal atmosphere to demand suffrage; progress would materialise in the 1970s.

## Challenges and Crises

### Constitutional Tensions

King Tribhuvan retained **constitutional veto power**. While he largely respected the parliamentary process during the early months, his advisors warned of potential instability.

### Economic Constraints

Nepal’s economy, still predominantly agrarian, struggled to fund ambitious development programs. International aid from India and the United Kingdom helped finance infrastructure projects, but fiscal deficits grew.

### Political Opposition

- **Rana Residuals:** Former Rana elites attempted to undermine reforms through legal challenges and media campaigns.
- **Left‑Wing Dissent:** A nascent **Communist Party of Nepal** (unified) organized protests demanding more radical redistribution and worker rights, culminating in the 1960 *student demonstrations*.

## The 1960 Royal Coup – A Prelude

The democratic momentum was abruptly halted by **King Mahendra’s 1960 royal coup**, dissolving parliament and establishing the **Panchayat system**. The 1959 election thus represents both the **high point** of Nepal’s first democratic experiment and a **prelude** to subsequent autocratic rule.

## Legacy of the 1959 Election

- **Institutional Foundations:** The election established a procedural template for future polls (1990, 2008). The **Election Commission** persisted as an independent body.
- **Political Culture:** The experience of competitive elections, even limited, fostered a political consciousness that resurfaced during later popular movements.
- **Historical Memory:** The 1959 election is commemorated each **February 18** as **Democratic Day** in Nepal, with academic conferences reflecting on its significance.
