# Quick Facts

- **Date:** 18 February 1959 – 4 May 1959 (election period)
- **Electoral System:** First‑past‑the‑post (FPTP) in 18 districts for the House of Representatives; 19 seats for the Senate appointed by the King.
- **Key Parties:** Nepali Congress (NC), Nepal Rashtrabadi Gorkha Parishad (NRGP), and independent candidates.
- **Turnout:** Approximately 43 % of eligible voters, around 1.3 million registered voters.
- **Winning Party:** Nepali Congress, securing 74 of the 109 contested seats.
- **Prime Minister‑Designate:** B.P. Koirala (first democratically elected Prime Minister of Nepal).
- **Significance:** Marked the first nationwide, multi‑party, parliamentary election in Nepal’s modern history, ending the era of appointed governments.

## Narrative Overview

### Historical Background

Following the *Dawn of Democracy* in 1951, Nepal entered a period of political fluidity. The interim government, under King Tribhuvan, oversaw the drafting of a constitution, which was finally promulgated in 1959. This constitution established a parliamentary monarchy with a bicameral legislature: the *House of Representatives* (lower house) and the *Senate* (upper house). It also introduced universal adult suffrage for citizens aged 21 and above—a radical departure from the limited franchise under the Rana regime.

### Campaign Dynamics

The Nepali Congress, led by B.P. Koirala, campaigned on a platform of **social justice**, **land reform**, and **civil liberties**. Their slogan, “*Sahaj‑Sanjeevan, Samajik Samabhav*” (Simple Life, Social Equality), resonated with the emerging urban middle class and the peasantry hungry for change.

The Nepal Rashtrabadi Gorkha Parishad (NRGP), a monarchist‑conservative party, positioned itself as the guardian of tradition, warning that rapid reforms could destabilise the nation. Smaller independent candidates represented regional interests, especially in the far‑west where the central government had limited reach.

Campaigning was conducted via **public rallies**, **radio broadcasts** on the state‑run Radio Nepal, and **political pamphlets** printed in Nepali and local dialects. Notably, the campaign coincided with a severe monsoon that disrupted transportation, limiting voter outreach in remote mountainous districts.

### Election Day and Results

Voting took place over several weeks to accommodate the country's geography. In Kathmandu, voter turnout was roughly 60 %, while remote districts such as **Humla** and **Mugu** saw turnout below 20 % due to logistical challenges.

The Nepali Congress overwhelmingly won in the more populated valleys and plains, while the NRGP secured victories primarily in the hilly districts of the central region. Independent candidates captured a handful of seats, reflecting localized support.

The final tally:

- **Nepali Congress:** 74 seats (68 % of the House)
- **NRGP:** 23 seats (21 %)
- **Independents/Others:** 12 seats (11 %)

Following the election, King Mahendra appointed B.P. Koirala as Prime Minister on **27 May 1959**, making him the first democratically elected head of government.

### Governance and Early Reforms

The newly elected government moved swiftly to implement its agenda:

1. **Land Reform Bill (1960)** – Intended to redistribute large estates owned by former Rana families to tenant farmers. Though the bill faced strong opposition and was only partially enacted, it laid groundwork for later agrarian reforms.
2. **Education Expansion** – The government doubled the number of primary schools and established the *University of Kathmandu* (later renamed *Tribhuvan University*) in 1959.
3. **Press Freedom Reinforcement** – Lifting of remaining censorship statutes, fostering a flourishing press corps.

These reforms, however, were short‑lived; a looming political crisis soon erupted.

## Socio‑Political Impact

- **Legitimisation of Democratic Institutions:** The election demonstrated that a multi‑party parliamentary system could function in Nepal, inspiring civil society and political activism.
- **Shift in Power Dynamics:** The Ranas, who had been sidelined, regained some influence through allied aristocratic politicians, but their power waned overall.
- **International Profile:** Nepal’s democratic experiment attracted foreign aid and diplomatic goodwill, particularly from India and the United States, who saw Nepal as a potential bulwark against communism.
- **Prelude to Autocratic Backlash:** The success of the NC alarmed King Mahendra, who later orchestrated a royal coup in 1960, dissolving parliament and curtailing democracy.
