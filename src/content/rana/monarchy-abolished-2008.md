# Monarchy Abolished (2008)

> *"When the constitution was signed, Nepal’s crown fell, giving rise to a republic that promised inclusive governance."

## Quick Facts

| Fact | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Date** | 28 May 2008 – Constituent Assembly declares Nepal a Federal Democratic Republic |
| **Location** | Kathmandu, Nepal – *International Convention Centre* (ICC), hosting the Constituent Assembly (CA) session |
| **Key Figures** | President *Ram Baran Yadav* (first president), Prime Minister *Pushpa Kamal Dahal* (Prachanda), former King *Gyanendra*, leaders of Nepali Congress, CPN‑UML, CPN‑M‑C
| **Constitution** | New constitution (2065 BS) promulgated, establishing a federal structure with 7 provinces |
| **Outcome** | End of the 240‑year Shah monarchy; establishment of a republican system with a president as head of state |

---

## Prelude to Abolition

The **People’s Movement II** (2006) forced King Gyanendra to relinquish absolute power and restore the *House of Representatives*. A *peace accord* with the Maoists (2006) led to the formation of a **Comprehensive Peace Agreement** and the inclusion of the CPN‑M‑C in the interim government.

A **Constituent Assembly (CA)** of **601 members** was elected in **April 2008** to draft a new constitution and decide the future of the monarchy. The CA was composed of a broad coalition: Nepali Congress, CPN‑UML, Madhesi parties, and the Maoists.

---

## The Historic Vote

On **28 May 2008**, after intense debates over federalism, secularism, and citizenship, the CA voted **560 yes** to **declare Nepal a republic** and **abolish the monarchy**. The vote was broadcast live on *Radio Nepal* and televised across the nation.

The **first President**, *Ram Baran Yadav* (a Nepali Congress stalwart), was elected on **23 July 2008**, completing the transition from a constitutional monarchy to a federal democratic republic.

---

## Constitutional Changes

The **2008 Constitution** (2065 BS) introduced:
1. **Federalism** – seven provinces with power devolution to provincial assemblies.
2. **Secularism** – state no longer endorses any religion.
3. **Citizenship Reform** – ensuring gender‑equal transmission of citizenship.
4. **Mixed Electoral System** – blending first‑past‑the‑post (FPTP) and proportional representation (PR).

---

## Social Impact

- **Women’s Rights**: The constitution enshrines **gender parity** in state bodies; subsequent elections saw a rise in women’s representation.
- **Madhesi Inclusion**: Federal restructuring addressed longstanding demands for representation from the *Madhesi* community.
- **Economic Outlook**: International donors, including the **World Bank**, pledged increased aid contingent on political stability.

---

## International Reaction

- **United Nations**: congratulated Nepal, emphasizing support for *peace, development, and human rights*.
- **India**: welcomed the move, noting that a *stable republic* would enhance bilateral cooperation.
- **European Union**: pledged **€120 million** for governance reforms and *post‑conflict reconstruction*.

---

## Legacy and Challenges

While the abolition marked a **break from centuries of monarchical rule**, the new republic faced challenges:
- **Political Fragmentation**: Frequent government changes due to coalition politics.
- **Constitution Implementation**: Ongoing debates about federal boundaries and powers.
- **Reconciliation**: Addressing wartime atrocities through the *Truth and Reconciliation Commission* (established 2015).

Nevertheless, the 2008 abolition remains a **defining moment** in Nepal’s trajectory toward inclusive democracy.
