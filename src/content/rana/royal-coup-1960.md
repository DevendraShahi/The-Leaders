# Royal Coup (1960)

> *"When the palace gates closed, the promises of democracy were locked away for a generation."

## Quick Facts

| Fact | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Date** | 15 December 1960 – King Mahendra declares a royal coup |
| **Location** | Kathmandu, Nepal – Royal Palace (Singha Durbar) and the *Parliament House* |
| **Key Figures** | King Mahendra, Crown Prince Bir Shamsher Jang Bahadur Rana, B.P. Koirala (deposed), Subarna Shamsher Rana |
| **Casualties** | Few – primarily political arrests; some reports of 2–3 protesters killed during street clashes |
| **Outcome** | Dissolution of parliament, banning of political parties, establishment of the *Panchayat* system, centralisation of power under the monarchy |

---

## Prelude: Growing Tensions

After the 1959 election, King Mahendra grew increasingly uneasy with the growing influence of the Nepali Congress and its reform agenda. The monarch feared that the elected government would diminish the traditional authority of the Shah dynasty and fracture Nepal’s fragile unity.

Mahendra’s advisors, many from the *Rana* aristocracy, argued that a strong monarchical hand was essential to maintain stability, especially given external pressures from India and the lingering Cold War rivalry.

---

## The Coup Execution

On **15 December 1960**, King Mahendra **suspended the constitution**, **dissolved the House of Representatives**, and **imprisoned key opposition leaders**, including B.P. Koirala and Subarna Shamsher Rana. The royal decree was announced via the state radio, *Radio Nepal*, declaring:
> "For the protection of the nation, the King of Nepal assumes direct authority."

Security forces, loyal to the monarchy, sealed off *Singha Durbar* and took control of the *Parliament House*. Within hours, the *Election Commission* was placed under royal supervision, and all political parties were ordered to cease activities.

---

## The Panchayat System Established

Mahendra introduced the **Panchayat system**, a party‑less “people’s council” structure meant to bypass partisan politics. The system comprised:
1. **Village Panchayats** – local bodies elected indirectly.
2. **District Panchayats** – aggregating village councils.
3. **National Panchayat** – the supreme legislative body, entirely appointed by the King.

The **1970 Constitution** later formalised the Panchayat model, granting the King absolute authority to appoint the *Prime Minister* and dissolve the *National Panchayat* at will.

---

## Domestic Impact

- **Political Repression**: Opposition parties were outlawed; leaders were placed under house arrest or exiled. The *Freedom of Press Act* (1961) imposed strict censorship; newspapers required royal approval before publication.
- **Socio‑Economic Policies**: The regime pursued a *“development‑first”* agenda, focusing on road construction (e.g., the *East‑West Highway*) and basic education. While infrastructure improved, wealth remained concentrated among the elite.
- **Cultural Control**: The monarchy promoted *“national unity”* narratives, emphasizing Nepali identity while suppressing ethnic minority movements.

---

## International Reactions

- **India** expressed concern but avoided direct confrontation, emphasizing non‑interference while maintaining diplomatic dialogue.
- **Britain** issued a terse statement acknowledging Nepal’s sovereign right to determine its political system.
- **United Nations** noted the *“suspension of democratic processes”* in its 1961 *Human Rights Report*, but refrained from taking punitive action.

---

## Resistance and Legacy

The coup sparked a **clandestine resistance** movement. Underground groups, including the *Communist Party of Nepal* and remnants of the *Nepali Congress*, organised sabotage campaigns and pamphleteering. These activities laid the foundations for the later *People’s War* (1996‑2006).

The Panchayat era persisted until **1990**, when a popular *People’s Movement* (Jana Andolan II) forced the restoration of multi‑party democracy.
