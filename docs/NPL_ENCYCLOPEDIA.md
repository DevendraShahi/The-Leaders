## 1. Executive Summary & League Overview

The **Nepal Premier League (NPL)** (नेपाल प्रिमियर लिग) is the premier professional Twenty20 (T20) cricket franchise league in Nepal, founded and governed directly by the **Cricket Association of Nepal (CAN)**. 

Established to professionalize Nepali domestic cricket, accelerate talent development, and capture the immense passion of Nepal’s millions of cricket supporters, the NPL has rapidly evolved into one of the most lucrative and internationally followed franchise tournaments among ICC Associate Nations.

Season 3 represents a watershed moment for the competition:
* **8 Provincially Rooted Franchises** competing across 32 total fixtures.
* **Over NPR 16.88 Crore ($1.26M+ USD)** raised solely from initial franchise auction bids.
* **NPR 3 Crore ($225,000+ USD)** total championship prize pool—the largest in Nepali sporting history.
* **Full DRS (Decision Review System)** with Hawk-Eye ball tracking and UltraEdge technology installed for every match.
* **International Linear Broadcast on Star Sports**, streaming globally on digital OTT networks, bringing global cricket icons (including David Warner, Shakib Al Hasan, David Miller, Andre Russell, Moeen Ali, and Jimmy Neesham) into direct competition with Nepal's national stars.

## 2. Historical Genesis & Tournament Evolution

### 2.1 The Predecessor Era (2016–2021)
Before the establishment of the centralized CAN-administered league, Nepali franchise cricket existed as fragmented, privately operated regional competitions:
1. **Dhangadhi Premier League (DPL):** Pioneered franchise cricket in the Far-West, initiating domestic auctions and player price brackets.
2. **Everest Premier League (EPL):** Hosted in Kirtipur; attracted the first wave of marquee overseas stars (Chris Gayle, Shahid Afridi, Corey Anderson, Richard Levi) and demonstrated massive stadium spectator demand.
3. **Pokhara Premier League (PPL):** Expanded professional franchise cricket to the Gandaki region at Pokhara Cricket Ground.

### 2.2 Rebirth Under CAN: The Official Nepal Premier League
Recognizing the commercial potential and the necessity for ICC compliance and anti-corruption oversight, CAN unified and centralized the domestic franchise structure into the official **Nepal Premier League (NPL)**:
* **Season 1 (Inaugural):** Captured by **Janakpur Bolts**, who established Madhesh Province's dominance with a blend of overseas tactical experience and young local batsmen.
* **Season 2:** Won by **Lumbini Lions**, led by national team captain Rohit Paudel, solidifying the league's high competitive parity.
* **Season 3 (2026):** The full commercial leap forward—characterized by historic franchise bidding fees, Star Sports broadcast expansion, stadium floodlights, and world-class squad depth.

## 4. Auction Mechanics, Salary Caps & Squad Regulations

### 4.1 Domestic Player Auction Categories
Domestic players are categorized into three distinct tiers with mandatory reserve prices and maximum bidding ceilings to prevent financial inflation:
* **Category A:** Base price: NPR 10 Lakh (1,000,000 NPR) · Maximum Bid Cap: **NPR 15 Lakh – 20 Lakh**. When multiple franchises reach the maximum cap for a player, ownership is awarded through an official blind lucky draw.
* **Category B:** Base price: NPR 5 Lakh · Ceiling range: **NPR 5 Lakh – 10 Lakh**.
* **Category C:** Base price: NPR 2 Lakh · Ceiling range: **NPR 2 Lakh – 5 Lakh**.

### 4.2 Squad & Playing XI Composition Rules
1. **Total Squad Size:** 16 to 19 players per franchise.
2. **Overseas Quota:**
   * Each squad is permitted up to **5 or 6 overseas players**.
   * In the **Playing XI**, a minimum and maximum of **4 overseas players** must feature in every match.
3. **Domestic Marquee Player:** Each franchise must sign one designated Nepali marquee player outside the general auction pool.
4. **Local / Emerging Player Mandate:**
   * Each franchise must recruit local talent from its respective province/region through dedicated talent hunts.
   * At least one emerging domestic player must feature in matchday squads to promote grassroots development.

## 6. Tournament Format & Rules of Play

### 6.1 Competition Format
* **League Stage:** Single round-robin format consisting of **28 matches**. Each of the 8 franchises plays the other 7 teams once.
* **Points System:**
  * **Win:** 2 points.
  * **Tie / No Result / Abandonment:** 1 point each.
  * **Loss:** 0 points.
  * **Tie-break:** Net Run Rate (NRR) determines ranking if points are level.
* **Page Playoff System (4 matches):**
  * **Qualifier 1:** 1st place vs 2nd place. Winner advances directly to Grand Finale; loser proceeds to Qualifier 2.
  * **Eliminator:** 3rd place vs 4th place. Winner proceeds to Qualifier 2; loser is eliminated.
  * **Qualifier 2:** Loser of Qualifier 1 vs Winner of Eliminator. Winner advances to Grand Finale.
  * **Grand Finale:** Winner of Qualifier 1 vs Winner of Qualifier 2 to determine the Champion.

### 6.2 ICC Playing Conditions & Technical Innovations
* **Full DRS (Decision Review System):** Hawk-Eye ball-tracking, UltraEdge snickometer, and dedicated third-umpire TV replays used across all 32 matches. Each team receives **two unsuccessful reviews per innings**.
* **Powerplay:** Overs 1 through 6 restricted to two fielders outside the 30-yard circle.
* **Short-Pitched Bowling:** Up to **two bouncers (shoulder-height)** permitted per over.
* **Strategic Timeouts:** Two 2.5-minute tactical timeouts per innings (one batting, one bowling).
* **Super Over:** Every tied match in both the league phase and playoffs is decided by a Super Over (and subsequent Super Overs if tied).

## 7. Match Venues & Stadium Infrastructure

### 7.1 Primary Venue: Tribhuvan University International Cricket Ground
* **Location:** Kirtipur, Kathmandu Valley (Elevation: 1,330 m / 4,360 ft above sea level)
* **Capacity:** ~20,000 spectators
* **Pitch Characteristics:**
  * Traditionally true bounce with excellent carry for strokeplayers during day fixtures.
  * Thin Himalayan air promotes high boundary counts and six-hitting.
  * Evening dew under floodlights assists seamers with new ball skiddiness and requires spinners to adapt grip.
* **Upgrades for Season 3:** Permanent LED floodlight pylons, high-definition broadcast towers, international-standard digital sight screens, and dedicated VIP/media pavilions.

### 7.2 Secondary & Training Grounds
* **Upper & Lower Mulpani Cricket Grounds:** International-standard training and auxiliary facilities in eastern Kathmandu.
* **Pokhara International Cricket Ground:** Serves as high-altitude regional training and future expansion match venue.
